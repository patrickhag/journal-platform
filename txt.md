    "abstract": "This short communication presents the development and field testing of a portable device for rapid malaria detection, demonstrating high sensitivity and specificity in rural clinics. Malaria remains a significant public health challenge in many regions, particularly in Sub-Saharan Africa, where timely and accurate diagnosis is critical for effective disease management and control. Traditional diagnostic methods, such as microscopy and rapid diagnostic tests (RDTs), often require laboratory infrastructure or trained personnel, which may not be readily available in remote or resource-limited settings. The portable device described in this report is designed to address these limitations by providing a user-friendly, battery-operated solution capable of delivering reliable results within minutes. Field evaluations were conducted in collaboration with local healthcare workers across multiple rural clinics, assessing the device’s performance under real-world conditions. Results indicate that the device achieved over 95% sensitivity and 97% specificity compared to gold-standard laboratory diagnostics. Additionally, healthcare workers reported high satisfaction with the device’s ease of use, portability, and minimal training requirements. The implementation of this technology has the potential to improve malaria case detection rates, facilitate prompt treatment, and ultimately contribute to reducing malaria-related morbidity and mortality. Further studies are recommended to evaluate long-term durability, cost-effectiveness, and integration into existing healthcare workflows. This report highlights the promise of innovative diagnostic tools in strengthening health systems and supporting global efforts to combat malaria.",
